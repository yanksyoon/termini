# Termini — Enterprise Certifications: leverage & design obligations

Status: draft — research complete. Owner: `pitch` (kanban task `t_6dc36450`).
Consumed by: W9 root synthesis (`t_149e890a`), `architect` design-control mapping (`t_9128b7b9`), and go-to-market positioning.
Sibling (read together): `docs/compliance/regulatory-landscape.md` — this file is its certification counterpart. Where the landscape maps *laws*, this file maps the *attestable certificates* that convert those laws into procurement shortlistings.
Posture under analysis: **customer data stays on customer hardware; Termini operates the fleet remotely** ("your hardware, your data, our operations"). The controller (GCP-hosted) is the only Termini-operated "cloud service"; the on-prem node is the customer's.

Citation convention: every certification links to its primary source (ISO catalogue page, AICPA/CIMA, CSA, ANSSI, BSI, HHS, FedRAMP PMO, StateRAMP, PCI SSC). Every "what the design must prove" names the concrete control in `docs/contracts/governance.md` / `docs/architecture.md` that already satisfies (or would need to satisfy) it — not a generic aspiration. Cost/time figures are **indicative published ranges** (practitioner/pricing sources), not quotes; scope, headcount and auditor choice move them materially. Where the Termini design **cannot** meet a requirement as-is, it is flagged in §4, not smoothed over.

## 2. Table-stakes vs differentiators (for the first-adopter segments)

**First-adopter segments** (from `docs/narrative.md` + `regulatory-landscape.md` §2): data-sensitive SMBs who can't ship data to a SaaS; regulated mid-market (finance, health, public sector); AI-platform buyers; EU data controllers; critical-sector SMEs.

**Table-stakes — the floor every enterprise sale expects, get these first:**
- **ISO/IEC 27001** and **SOC 2 Type II** (Security + Confidentiality). These two are the non-negotiable baseline a vendor-security questionnaire opens with. Everything else in the table *reuses their evidence*, so they are the correct first investment.
- **CSA STAR Level 2** and **EU Cloud CoC adherence** ride on that same evidence for near-zero incremental cost — do them *with*, not *after*, the base certs.

**Differentiators — what actually wins a deal in Termini's segments:**
- **ISO/IEC 42001** is the standout. It certifies *the exact thing Termini sells* (a governed AI operator), few competitors hold it, and it pre-arms the EU AI Act risk-management conversation. Highest differentiation-per-dollar; pursue it as soon as ISO 27001 is in flight.
- **ISO/IEC 27701** reinforces "your data never leaves" for privacy-sensitive buyers at low marginal cost, because Termini's PII surface is provably minimal (C0/C1-only).

**Segment-specific unlocks (defer until a named deal justifies them):**
- **C5** for German buyers; **HITRUST + a BAA** for US healthcare; **PCI DSS** only if a payment-adjacent customer appears.
- **SecNumCloud** and **FedRAMP/StateRAMP** are the two *structural* (not controls) bets: SecNumCloud needs an EU-incorporated, EU-majority-owned entity with EU-resident operations; FedRAMP/StateRAMP need a full 800-53 authorization package for the GCP controller. Both are multi-quarter, six-figure commitments — treat them as **revenue-gated**, not "certify early."

**Sequencing recommendation:** ISO 27001 → (in parallel) SOC 2 Type II observation window + CSA STAR cross-walk + EU Cloud CoC → ISO 42001 → ISO 27701. Then segment overlays (C5 / HITRUST / PCI) only when a specific first-adopter signals them.

## 4. Open gaps (design cannot satisfy these as-is — flagged, not glossed)

- **G-SNC — SecNumCloud sovereignty chapter is a corporate blocker, not a controls gap.** The v3.2 référentiel requires an EU-incorporated provider with EU-majority capital and EU-resident operations/support, immune to extraterritorial law. Termini's current structure does not meet this. Owner: founders/legal — do not sell into French sovereign deals until resolved.
- **G-CERT1 — No shipped DPA/SCC/sub-processor artifacts.** ISO 27701, EU Cloud CoC, and the SOC 2 Confidentiality/Privacy story all presuppose a signed processor contract package. Mirrors gaps G1/G8 in `regulatory-landscape.md` §4. Owner: legal/commercial.
- **G-CERT2 — No BAA.** HITRUST/HIPAA positioning requires a business-associate agreement before any US healthcare customer runs ePHI workloads. Owner: legal/commercial.
- **G-CERT3 — FedRAMP/StateRAMP govern the controller (GCP), which has no 800-53 SSP today.** The on-prem node helps the *customer's* boundary but the controller is the CSO in scope. Mirrors gap G10. Owner: security/infra — revenue-gated.
- **G-CERT4 — AI management artifacts (ISO 42001) are unbuilt.** The `guardrailClamp → commandAllowlist` evidence exists but there is no AI policy, no per-system risk/impact assessment, and no documented AIMS roles yet. Owner: product/security.
- **G-CERT5 — Cost/time figures are indicative ranges, not quotes.** All §1 cost/time cells are published practitioner/pricing ranges; obtain itemized quotes from accredited bodies (ISO) / licensed CPA firms (SOC 2) / authorized assessors (HITRUST) before committing budget.
